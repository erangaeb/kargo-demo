**Overview:**

Hungary utilizes a real-time reporting system for invoices managed by the National Tax and Customs Administration of Hungary (NAV), known as the Online Számla system. While electronic invoicing itself isn't strictly mandatory in the sense that businesses *must* issue invoices electronically, the *reporting* of invoice data to NAV is mandatory for virtually all B2B and B2C transactions. This system aims to increase transparency and facilitate VAT-related data reporting.

**Key Aspects:**

*   **Governing Body:** National Tax and Customs Administration of Hungary (NAV) (Nemzeti Adó és Vámhivatal)
*   **System:** NAV Online Számla (Online Invoice System)
*   **Mandatory Reporting:** Real-Time Information Reporting (RTIR) is mandatory for all VAT-registered and resident taxpayers in Hungary.
*   **Scope:**  Applies to all B2B and B2C transactions.
*   **Effective Date:** RTIR was initially introduced on July 1, 2018, and became mandatory for all B2B and B2C transactions from April 1, 2021.
*   **E-Invoicing Mandate:** While not a direct mandate to issue e-invoices, the mandatory real-time reporting to NAV effectively necessitates the use of electronic systems for invoice management.

**Format and Transmission:**

*   **Invoice Formats:**
    *   **For Reporting to NAV:** XML format is required for submitting invoice data to the NAV Online Számla system.
    *   **For Exchange with Buyers/Recipients:** Invoices can be exchanged with buyers/recipients in various formats, including paper, PDF, or XML.  The key is that the data must also be reported to NAV in the required XML format.
*   **Transmission:** Invoice data must be transmitted electronically to the NAV Online Számla system.

**Compliance Requirements:**

*   **VAT Registration:**  The system applies to all VAT-registered and resident taxpayers in Hungary.
*   **Real-Time Reporting:** Invoice data must be reported to NAV in real-time or near real-time.
*   **Data Requirements:** Specific data elements are required in the XML format for reporting to NAV.  These likely include (but are not limited to):
    *   Supplier and buyer details (name, address, tax ID)
    *   Invoice number
    *   Invoice date
    *   Description of goods or services
    *   Quantity
    *   Unit price
    *   Net amount
    *   VAT rate
    *   VAT amount
    *   Gross amount
*   **XML Schema:**  NAV provides a specific XML schema that must be followed when submitting invoice data.  This schema defines the structure and data types of the required information.
*   **Authentication:**  Secure authentication methods are used to ensure the integrity and security of the data transmitted to NAV.

**Process Summary:**

1.  **Invoice Creation:** The supplier creates an invoice (either electronically or manually).
2.  **Data Extraction:** If the invoice is created manually (e.g., on paper), the relevant data must be extracted and converted into the required XML format.
3.  **Data Transmission:** The XML data is transmitted to the NAV Online Számla system.
4.  **NAV Validation:** NAV validates the data against the XML schema and other compliance rules.
5.  **Invoice Delivery:** The invoice (in the agreed-upon format, e.g., PDF, paper, or XML) is delivered to the buyer.

**Implications:**

*   **Automation:** The mandatory reporting requirement encourages businesses to automate their invoicing processes to ensure timely and accurate data submission.
*   **Integration:** Businesses need to integrate their accounting or ERP systems with the NAV Online Számla system to facilitate seamless data transmission.
*   **Transparency:** The real-time reporting system provides NAV with greater visibility into VAT transactions, helping to combat tax evasion.
