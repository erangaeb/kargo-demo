Okay, here's a consolidated summary of the eInvoicing/digital reporting landscape in Hungary, based on all the provided documents:

**Key Takeaways:**

* **Real-time Reporting (RTIR) is Mandatory:** Hungary uses a centralized, real-time invoice reporting system (NAV Online Számla) managed by the National Tax and Customs Administration of Hungary (NAV). All VAT-registered and resident taxpayers must report invoice data to NAV in real-time. This applies to both B2B and B2C transactions, domestic and cross-border.
* **eInvoicing is Voluntary (But RTIR is Key):** While electronic invoicing itself isn't mandated, the real-time reporting requirement effectively pushes businesses towards electronic solutions.
* **NAV Online Számla Platform:** This is the central platform for reporting.
* **XML Format is Crucial:** Invoice data must be reported to NAV in NAV XML format (version 3.0 or later).
* **Registration and Technical User Setup Required:** Businesses must register on the Online Számla platform and create technical users and keys to connect their ERP/accounting systems.
* **Archiving:** Invoices must be archived for 8 years for accounting purposes (5 years standard). Archiving abroad is allowed under certain conditions.
* **Penalties for Non-Compliance:** Failure to comply with RTIR can result in significant fines (up to HUF 500,000 per unreported or incorrectly reported invoice).
* **SAF-T:** Not currently required, but expected to be introduced.
* **No eSignature Required:** eSignatures are not required for the XML format.
* **Buyer's Consent Required:** Buyer's consent is required for receiving e-invoices.

**In essence, while you don't *have* to issue an eInvoice, you *must* report invoice data electronically and in real-time to the NAV using the specified XML format.** This makes having a system that can generate and transmit compliant XML files essential for doing business in Hungary.
